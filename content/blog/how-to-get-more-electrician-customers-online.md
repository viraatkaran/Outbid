An electrical job is rarely planned in advance. A breaker keeps tripping, a landlord needs a safety check before a new tenant moves in, or a homeowner has just bought an EV and wants a charger fitted before the weekend. In every case, the person searching picks from whoever shows up first, looks trustworthy and answers the phone. Getting more electrician customers online means winning that moment, again and again, rather than chasing one-off jobs.

## Start with the Google Business Profile, not a fancy website

Before a new website, a new logo or a paid ad, fix the free listing most searchers see first.

- **Pick the right categories.** "Electrician" as the primary category, with secondary categories for anything you actually do (EV charger installation, electrical inspection, home automation).
- **List your real service area**, not just your office suburb — most searches are "electrician near me" or "[suburb] electrician", and the profile needs to match both.
- **Add photos of real work**: switchboards, EV chargers, finished panels. A profile with recent photos and a steady stream of reviews outperforms a polished but static website on its own.
- **Keep hours and emergency availability accurate.** Nothing loses a job faster than a profile that says "open" at 11pm for a business that does not answer after 6pm.

For the fuller version of this, see our [local SEO checklist for small businesses](/blog/local-seo-checklist-for-small-businesses) — most of it applies directly to a trades business.

## Chase the higher-margin jobs, not just more calls

Not every electrical call is worth the same. Panel upgrades, rewires, solar connections and EV charger installs typically pay more per visit than a single socket repair, and the people searching for them tend to have already decided they need a professional rather than shopping purely on price.

- **Write one page for EV charger installation**, separate from your general services page. Cover what's involved (load calculation, permit or inspection requirements where they apply, typical timeframes), and be upfront that you cannot quote an exact price without seeing the property.
- **Write one page for panel or switchboard upgrades**, aimed at people who have been told by an inspector or insurer that an upgrade is needed.
- **Mention brands and standards you actually work with** (charger brands you're certified to install, relevant local wiring standards) — specific, checkable details build more trust than generic claims.
- Never promise a fixed price, a guaranteed timeframe or a government rebate you haven't personally confirmed still applies; electrical incentive programmes change often and a wrong claim damages trust fast.

## Build trust with evidence, not adjectives

Nobody can see electrical work once the wall is closed up, so trust has to come from somewhere else.

- **Ask for a review immediately after the job**, while the customer still remembers how it went, and reply to every review — good or bad.
- **Show your licence, registration and insurance clearly** on your site and profile. For regulated electrical work, this is often the single biggest trust signal you have.
- **Use real before-and-after photos** of switchboards, wiring and finished installs rather than stock images.
- Never buy reviews, write your own, or ask customers to post reviews that aren't genuine — platforms detect patterns like this and can suspend a listing, which costs far more than the review was worth.

## Be where people look besides search

Search is only one channel. Combine it with:

- **Trade and industry directories** relevant to electrical contractors in your country.
- **Local community groups and neighbourhood apps**, where people regularly ask "does anyone know a good electrician" — answer helpfully and follow each group's rules on self-promotion.
- **Ranking boards for your country and category.** You can see how electricians are ranked in [the United Kingdom](/c/united-kingdom/electrician) and in [Australia](/c/australia/electrician), and claim a category-level spot in your own country for a low starting price to appear alongside the businesses already competing for attention there.
- **Partnerships with related trades** — builders, solar installers and property managers who need an electrician they can recommend without hesitation.

## Respond fast, especially for urgent jobs

Electrical emergencies (no power, a tripped main, sparking outlets) create the same urgency as a burst pipe. Our guide on [getting more plumbing customers online](/blog/how-to-get-more-plumbing-customers-online) covers the same principle in more depth: a missed-call text, a clear emergency number, or a promise like "we call back within 30 minutes" often wins the job over a competitor who simply answers slower.

## Track what actually brings in work

Resist the urge to be everywhere at once. Pick a small number of channels — your Google profile, one or two directories, one ranking board — and track which ones produce real booked jobs, not just clicks or calls that go nowhere.

- Use a separate phone number or tracking link per channel where you can.
- Ask every new customer, directly, how they found you.
- After a month, compare what each channel produced against the time or money it cost. Our explainer on [whether paid leaderboards actually work](/blog/paid-leaderboards-do-they-work) walks through this kind of comparison in more detail.
- Drop what brings nothing after a fair trial, and put more effort into what works.

## What to do this week

Pick three things: finish and correct your Google Business Profile, write one dedicated page for your highest-margin service (EV chargers or panel upgrades are a good place to start), and ask your last five customers for a review.
