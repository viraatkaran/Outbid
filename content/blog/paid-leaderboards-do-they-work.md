Pay-to-rank pages are cheap to try, easy to understand and fun to share, so they are tempting. But do paid leaderboards work? The honest answer is that they can, for the right business, if you treat them as one small channel and measure the result. They are not a shortcut around building something people want.

## What a paid leaderboard actually is

On a paid-placement leaderboard, businesses pay to appear, and position is decided by how much each one has paid. The page is closer to a billboard than to a review site. A high rank means someone chose to spend, not that an independent judge picked the best business. Honest sites say so openly. Ours labels every listing "paid placement" and publishes the exact [ranking rules](/rules).

Understanding this protects both sides. Visitors know what they are looking at, and advertisers set the right expectations.

## What a paid leaderboard can do

- **Send a small amount of targeted traffic.** People browsing a list for a category or country are already interested in that kind of business.
- **Add a link and a mention.** Some listings give you a link from a real page, which helps people and search engines find you. Treat any SEO benefit as a bonus, not the goal.
- **Create social proof and a moment to share.** Reaching the top of a local board gives you something concrete to post about.
- **Give you data.** Verified clicks tell you how much attention your listing really gets.

## What it cannot do

- It cannot fix a weak product or a confusing website.
- It cannot promise sales. Clicks are not customers.
- It will not replace the basics: a clear offer, good reviews, and a website that loads fast and explains what you do.

## Why local and category boards matter

A single worldwide list is crowded and expensive. Narrower boards are cheaper and closer to your real customers. A plumber in Auckland gains little from a global ranking but a lot from being near the top of the plumbing board for New Zealand. On BillboardRank you can compete at three levels: a category inside one country, a whole country, or worldwide. You can see how this looks for yourself on the [countries page](/countries).

## How to measure the return

Treat this like any experiment. Before you pay, decide what success looks like.

1. **Add tracking.** Use a link with campaign tags, or check your analytics for visits from the listing.
2. **Count verified clicks.** Verified clicks remove bots and repeat clicks from the same visitor on the same day. Our dashboard shows both verified and raw numbers, so you can see the difference.
3. **Count outcomes.** How many of those visitors signed up, called, booked or bought?
4. **Do the sum.** Divide what you spent by the number of outcomes. If a placement cost 50 dollars and brought 5 sign-ups, each sign-up cost 10 dollars.
5. **Compare.** Is that better or worse than your other channels? Keep what beats them and drop what does not.

## A worked example

Imagine a dental clinic in Mumbai pays 24 dollars to appear near the top of its category board. Over a month the listing receives 60 verified clicks. Fifteen of those visitors read the pricing page, and three book a check-up worth 40 dollars each. Revenue from the listing is 120 dollars against a 24 dollar cost, so each booking cost 8 dollars to win. That is a good result, and the clinic can safely test a higher position. If instead the same 60 clicks had produced no bookings, the clinic would know the problem was its page or its offer, not the ranking, and could fix that before spending more.

## Common mistakes

- **Paying to be first and stopping there.** Rank only helps if the page people land on is clear.
- **Judging after one day.** Give a listing at least a few weeks before you decide.
- **Ignoring country and category.** Small, specific boards are often better value than the biggest one.
- **Trusting raw click counts.** Always ask how a number was verified.

## Red flags in any ranking site

Be cautious if a site hides that rankings are paid, shows traffic numbers that are never explained, promises guaranteed sales or offers to sell you fake reviews. A trustworthy site shows its rules, its prices and its method.

## A sensible way to start

Start small. Pick the narrowest board that matches your customers, pay the starting price, watch the verified clicks for a month, and only then decide whether to spend more.
