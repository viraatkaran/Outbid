When someone nearby searches for a service, the businesses that appear first get the call. A good local SEO checklist turns that search into customers without needing a large budget. Every step below is free or low cost, and you can finish most of them in a weekend. Work through them in order and you will be ahead of most competitors in your area.

## Part 1: Get your basics exactly right

**1. Choose one consistent name, address and phone number.** Write your business name, street address and phone number exactly the same way everywhere. Small differences, like "Street" in one place and "St" in another, confuse search engines and customers.

**2. Claim and complete your Google Business Profile.** This is usually the single most important local listing. Add your correct categories, opening hours, service area, photos and a short description. Keep the hours up to date, including holidays.

**3. Claim the same details on the other main map and search services.** Apple Maps and Bing Places are free and take minutes to set up.

**4. Make a fast, simple website.** Your site needs your services, your area, your contact details and a clear call to action on the first screen. It must work well on a phone, because most local searches happen there.

## Part 2: Match what people search for

**5. Write one page per main service.** A page called "Emergency plumbing in Wellington" will match a search better than a single page that lists everything.

**6. Mention your area naturally.** Use the suburb, city and region where customers are, in headings and text, without repeating it unnaturally.

**7. Add real details.** Prices or price ranges, how long jobs take, and answers to common questions make your pages more useful than a competitor's generic page.

**8. Write good page titles and descriptions.** Put the service and place near the start of the title, and write a short description that makes people want to click.

## Part 3: Build trust

**9. Ask every happy customer for a review.** Send a short, direct link after the job is done. Reply to every review, good or bad, politely and quickly. Never buy reviews or write fake ones, because platforms detect them and can remove your listing.

**10. Show real photos.** Photos of your team, vehicles, shop and finished work build trust faster than stock images.

**11. List your business in good directories.** Choose directories that are real and relevant: your industry body, your chamber of commerce, and local business listings. A few good listings beat hundreds of poor ones. You can also appear on a country and category board, such as [plumbing in New Zealand](/c/new-zealand/plumbing), where visitors are already looking for your kind of business.

**12. Earn links from local sources.** Sponsor a local team, speak at an event, or partner with a neighbouring business. A mention on a local website is worth more than dozens of random links. Never buy links, because that breaks search engine rules and can get your site demoted.

## Part 4: Keep it working

**13. Post an update regularly.** Share a recent job, an offer or a seasonal tip once or twice a month on your profile and website.

**14. Track the right things.** Count calls, form messages, direction requests and bookings, not just website visits. Add a tracking number or tag to links so you know which listing sent each customer. Our post on [measuring paid placements](/blog/paid-leaderboards-do-they-work) shows a simple method.

**15. Review the list every quarter.** Check that your details are still correct everywhere, add new photos, and respond to any review you missed.

## How long does it take?

Fixing the basics can bring results within weeks. Reviews and links build up over months. The businesses that win locally are rarely the ones with the cleverest tricks. They are the ones that stay accurate, helpful and visible for a long time.
